It is less than stellar, actually.

First, I do not get why some of the notable characters in the film are
*still* scrambling for the approval of Miranda Priestly (played by Meryl
Streep), twenty years after the events of the first film. No, not
because Miranda is the editor-in-chief, which would mean, of course, she
needs to approve and review everything before it gets published. By
approval, I mean recognition, as in "I see that you are doing good
work." Well, maybe *scrambling* is too strong a word. Perhaps,
*thirsty*.

Not only that, they are actively enabling Miranda to do her subtle ways
of stepping over people (figuratively) instead of speaking up. I suppose
I just chalk it up to, "This is how it is in the competitive industry of
fashion," and "Miranda is visionary." Why must people endure her
"genius" at the expense of their mental and emotional well-being?

I am likely missing some things here. This movie and the first one are
based on a book of the same name, which I have not read. There are
likely details in the book that may not have been adapted into the film.
None of that matters, though, because I am critiquing the films. So, I
ask: "Are people around Miranda hopelessly devoted to her in a strange
Stockholm-syndrome way?" A hyperbolic, rhetorical question, certainly,
because Miranda herself is still participating in hierarchical
corporate [capitalism](/capitalism), which means, as much as people over
whom she has power continue to lick her boots[^boots] squeaky clean, she
herself must also lick the boots of people who have more money and
influence than her.

[^boots]: The brands of which I have not managed to find in my research
for this review.

A big plot twist also seemed hand-wavy and not well written. It is as if
the writer(s) had gotten tired of the story, and just incorporated the
"Shit happens unexpectedly sometimes" trope and developed the rest of
the story from that.

If there is one good takeaway from this, let it be: when given the
runway they deserve, women can, do, and will soar; and some can
even accomplish that without having to take others for granted.
