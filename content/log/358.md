(*or some 450-word reflection on reacting to hot takes*)

I am always tempted to react to (what I think are) stupid things people
say and post online, mostly on large [social media
platforms](/social-media). To be fair, social media's algorithms as
they are now reward absurd performances. When reactions to performances
are perfomances themselves, they also tend to gain traction, thus
creating a feedback loop that reinforces attention retention. Just a
cursory search on YouTube for the phrase "reaction video [on a
topic/artist/film/book/piece of media you love]" will yield infinite
results of people acting out their thoughts on camera, literally
performing for an audience. Now, I would say that my exposure to social
media is limited compared to many people, but still nontrivial. Having
said all that, I am not above its pull. Meaning, if and when I encounter
somebody's "hot takes," there is an impulse in me to combust externally;
the hotter the take, the more violent my reaction, the more I
want---*need*---to make my contradiction known to the public. I am not
out of the feedback loop.

So, what I would do is take an intersting hot take and turn it into a
writing prompt. I would then wrangle my wits for a comeback. Sometimes
short and sweet, sometimes long and lingering. Many times, though, I
would just move on from the exercise, glad that I have now some patience
and wisdom to not add to the din, because it is not worth it.

I wonder sometimes whether such hot takes double as ragebait not to
be separated from the whole performance. And as I "wrangle my wits," I
also pause to ask: Should I yuck their yum? To what extent? And at what
cost?

In some cases, I think it is a worthwhile exercise to try and offer an
alternate opinion, especially if such ragebaits promote harmful hate
speech, which can and does come from all political (and apolitical)
stances. In my experience, ideas expressed with as much grace as I can
muster tend to get considered more. And like I said, I am not outside
the loop: I dream that my alternate opinion would be life-changing for
those blessed enough to read it if I shared it. But that is simply
delusional in the age of [internet](/internet).

Anyway, the process of yucking one's yum need not be a toxic endeavor
for everyone involved. It can be reframed as providing a different
perspective---perhaps a kinder yet sharper analysis, a more balanced
take, even a more humane retort by putting forth a "what it feels like
in these shoes"---because we cannot just let feelings skew
[nihilistic](/nihilism) if we ever hope for [a meaningful
change](/revolution) on everything everywhere all at once.
