## Accountability

In English (roughly), "We are all accountable to one another."

It is a line from a short Roman Catholic song. While not a religious
person myself, I sang it recently with other people, for the
first death anniversary of [an uncle](/280). The observance is
called "Babang Luksa", itself a part of mourning tradition; and from
what I know, it consists of a prayer service for
the dead one year after they passed. As with any gathering, it is also
an excuse to reunite with and get updates from attending relatives.

In my opinion, the [subject] line above is the *only* key takeaway from
that song (and frankly, from all the songs we sung), which is
interesting because it goes beyond the [religion](/religion) and
transcends into [social ecology](/social-ecology). I do believe that
everything is connected, all our lives, flora and fauna and everything
in between, are intertwined. All our freedoms matter only if we are all
free.

A year is both too short and too long a time for a meaningful change in
these chaotic times, as we all try to find meaning in condolences
offered to our tiny little deaths. We die a little at the expense of
looking for lives we seek to live/leave.

## Thoughts on *Delikado* (2022)

Do the large, ancient trees in Palawan being illegally cut down affect
me in any way? Yes, of course.

The environmental documentary [*Delikado*](https://www.delikadofilm.com/) follows the
struggles of environmental defenders of Palawan forests and seas against
the powers-that-be that want to extract profit from:

- illegal logging of hardwood;
- illegal fishing through the use of cyanide and/or dynamites; and
- mining that carves Indegenous People's ancestral domains.

[Palawan NGO Network Inc.](https://www.pnni.org/) (PNNI), up until the
completion of the film, had been seizing the tools of illegal activities
as their way of fighting them. The defenders had confiscated chainsaws and
flatboats from illegal loggers and fishermen respectively. This tactic
of disabling oppressors and opportunists by ridding them of their tools
[has long been in use by radical activists](/direct-action) everywhere.

Equally worth noting: the defenders do not employ carcerality
(in whatever form) as a strategy. Granted, what they are doing is
[citizen's arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen%27s_arrest).
While the defenders do not enjoy the same immunity as police officers
do, this also means they could choose to be less menacing and less violent.
This was evident in the confrontations between
the defenders and the loggers and fishermen, at least the ones shown in
the film, that always began with deescalation, while
simultaneously confiscating and disarming the tools.

Naturally, PNNI is met with threats to their lives. The film made us
realize that since Rodrigo Duterte's presidency, the culture of impunity
has been normalized into a long nightmare.

## *Trapo*

We recently went to karaoke with friends to celebrate a birthday. I was
pleasantly surprised when one of us reserved the song [*Trapo* by Yano](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_q9n5CBQPNQ) (YouTube).
More than three decades since its release, the song is unfortunately
still relevant in today's Philippine politics.

Trapo means traditional politician, and is meant as derogatory.
