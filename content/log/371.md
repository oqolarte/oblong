Eve Macarro (played by Ana de Armas), who could be on her way to being
the female counterpart of John Wick, might never escape the life set for
her. This all depends, of course, on the whims of the producers of the
John Wick offshoot film. For all we know, killers will rain down upon
her now that---*spoiler*---a hefty bounty is placed on her head at the
very end of the film, which probably means more action in the coming
years.

Speaking of which, the action sequences are good, which is to be
expected from a film in John Wick lore. Two scenes stand out, both
arriving as surprises when viewers are led to believe they are in a safe
space---effectively conveying how the main antagonists operate. Expect
Brutality salted and peppered with the right amount of shock and gore.
*Ballerina*, after all, showcases [violence](/violence).

However, the fight scenes alone cannot save the plot. It is okay, but if
the producers were planning to jumpstart an entire saga out of this one,
they need to write a more compelling follow-up.

At the very least, I am genuinely glad there is no trace of copaganda as
far as I know. Nowhere in this two-hour girl-versus-all will you find a
law enforcer of any sort, not that they are needed (they are not). And
unlike the media that do lean into copaganda, the resolution, if one
could call it that, does not come from someone (read: police) cosplaying
as a purveyor of state-sanctioned justice. No, Eve Macarro, like John
Wick, serves justice cold and bloody.

This film, as well as the rest of the John Wick films, insinuates that
elite levels of gun fu cannot come solely from state-sponsored training.
Within its lore, the cult[ure]s of highly capable assassins have
preceded it. Complex rules of do's and don'ts guide them, but they are
born-and-bred killers---not even the "No business on Continental
grounds" rule could have prevented Death (literal or otherwise). Rules
matter even in vicious underground societies, yes, *until* vengeance
washes away old gripes.
