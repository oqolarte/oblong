Zach Cregger (director of *Weapons* (2025)) made this film a lot better
than previous takes. This latest iteration of the survival horror
franchise incorporates concepts from the game (of the same name) and
presents them in an exciting and relatable way. Exciting, because we
cannot look away from danger. And relatable, because Bryan (the
protagonist) is an ordinary, albeit clumsy, worker who literally has
neither training nor expertise in combat and guns, and somehow he must
survive the night in Raccoon City.

Cregger's *Resident Evil* has none of the familiar supercops in it, but
it is still grounded in the same claustrophobic settings that the game
is known for, like sewers and tunnels.

Aside from locations, we also observe the familiar blockers: stumbling
on locks and looking for their keys; needing guns and finding bullets;
and of course, shooting locks with guns, a thing only someone steeped in
action movies and games in pop culture would do.

All of it, though, is set in the US of A, where holding a gun is akin to
being a step within a big portal to ultraviolence. I say this because
Bryan is more than okay to shoot his first "zombie" with the explicit
goal of killing her. Being trigger happy, I guess, is more believable
when a white man is holding a gun than maiming or incapacitating, at
least for the first body count.

Watching Bryan, I think that carrying a dedicated flashlight in real
life is really useful. He keeps on pulling out his phone and using that
as his flashlight, when a dedicated one would have saved him precious
seconds in the high-stakes scenarios he finds himself in.
