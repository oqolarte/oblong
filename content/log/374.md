## Notes on *Banksy: You Are An Acceptable Level of Threat*

Banksy, the anonymous British street artist, has created lots of visual
commentary on surveillance that has gripped the UK. On multiple
occasions, he performed near cameras while staying just out of frame.

This relationship between artist and camera is not unique to Banksy.
Today, performing for the camera has become something many people do,
often yielding various results depending on the audience. For private
viewing, it is a captured slice of spacetime meant for friends and
family. For public, it is submitting to unsolicited whims of the
multitude whose lives you will never know, but whose attention, no
matter how brief, feeds your ego.

The performance, when mixed with the intention of the viewer, yields a
different range of effects. In some cases, it may inspire some to act on
their worst impulses. When people perform for the camera so that the
public might see it, the technology that facilitates it is that of both
[advertising](/no-ads) and [surveillance](/surveillance). This is
especially true when such public performance purports to propagate
propaganda for a grassroots-led movement. Surveillance capitalism, and
indeed [capitalism](/capitalism) as a whole, is so hungry for resources
that it consumes the purest sugar-free, grass-free, open-source
Revolution™.

If performance is easily co-opted, why do people still do it? It is
because sharing ideas is at the heart of wanting to perform. Poets write
carefully chosen phrases to express romance. Street artists beautify an
otherwise drab facade. Even politicians cry fake tears or wear fake
injuries on TV just to plead not guilty in front of millions. The
[technology](/technology) through which the messages travel is
definitely important. Who owns the technology has the advantage of
controlling the final output.

I think Banksy, through his art, is telling us: Speak freely, outside
the box the those in charge have forced us into.
