## Electricity costs

According to the International Energy Agency "[Key Questions on Energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai)", on 2025 data centers
used around around 485 terawatt-hours. that is equivalent to annual
electricity generation of germany.

While energy efficiency of AI has improved over the years, newer
energy-intensive AI applications are being launched and used, such as
those for video generation, reasoning, and agentic tasks.

## Water consumption

On the claim of Bases Conversion and Development Authority
([BCDA](https://www.bcda.gov.ph/)) on water use---if we look towards the
data center industry reporting on water use, specifically the types of
water sources, ~60% comes from potable water sources, that includes
surface water and ground water.  Rainwater harvesting, treatment and
water recycling are water conservation approaches, not sources of
unlimited water. The water has to come from somewhere initially right so
it could divert existing downstream flows of water, such as community
water.

Also, under the PH water code, there is a legal disincentive for
establishments to conserve water. In a water permit, the National Water
Resources Board (NWRB) includes
the total cubic meter of water you must consume. Under the beneficial
use doctrine, establishments that do not utilize the stated water volume
in a permit, will lose the right to use that water volume.  So if you
are a data center operating under PH laws, you have a water permit to
use an X amount of water. There is thus a massive incentive to utilize
all of your water allocation, even if in the course of operations, you
realize you do not need that much water; *or* even through your own
conservation efforts you end up using less water, you will still have to
use your water allocation as stated in your water permit, no more and no
less---just to protect yourself from the regulatory risk of losing your
water permit in whole or in part.

Data centers can be designed to significantly reduce water consumption,
reportedly up to 60%, but AI customers in data centers generally prefer
water cooling as opposed to other solutions focused on air-cooling.
Contrary to popular belief, data centers do not need to stay that cool
(20-25 degrees celsius).
