> It's so hard to love our country [[the Philippines](/pilipinas)]

... is a statement made by people, usually liberal-leaning in their
[politics](/politics), who somewhere in their heart of hearts believe
that patriotism is one of the keys to national progress. It is not.

I have observed that such people make such statements out of
frustration. And living in these times is really frustrating, that much
is valid.

But first of all, what does "a country" really mean, and what does it
encompass? Is it the entire biomass enclosed within an imaginary
geopolitcal border? Does it also include the State that continues to not
only disappoint but actively harm most of the living things under its
constituency? Such a weird scope for love, if you were to ask me.

I would also question whether patriotism is even a version of
[love](/love) when it is upscaled to multitudes of strangers and strange
things bound only by said imaginary geopolitical line. Not only that
such "love" is spread too thin, thus unsustainable, it is also
weaponized by the State to punish dissent, and optimized by Capitalists
to extract and funnel wealth from the land and its peoples to few
elites.

So for now, I humbly suggest, let us focus on caring for our chosen
families, [friends](/friendship), and [communities](/community),
wherever they may be.
