## "Don't look back"

The video essay "[Why Do So Many Myths Say 'DON’T LOOK BACK'?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ug4NZNWS7yk)" by The Three Pillars discusses such myths and I found them interesting.

### Orpheus

Read [Ovid's version of the story](https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/Latin/Metamorph10.php#anchor_Toc64105565).

When Eurydice died unexpectedly, Orpheus goes to the Underworld to beg
Hades to bring her back to life. Hades grants him the wish on the
condition that Orpheus must not look back or "the gift would be null and
void."

At the opening of the Underworld, but not quite out yet, Orpheus looks
back, only to see her wife recede back to the void from whence they
came, dying a second and final time.

An interpretation of this is about grief and moving on. It is only
natural for us to mourn our loved ones when they die, but at some point,
life must go on. We carry on, but how?

Another analysis involves humans having the natural tendency to doubt,
because in doubting the unknown, we dare verify. The act of
double-checking is crucial to the survival of our species. But in this
story of Orpheus, doubting, when directed at the "divine" and/or
"supernatural" can lead to more negative effects. Sometimes, it seems,
it is wiser to let Nature do its thing. Not everything needs human
intervention. Not everything needs an explanation, because the Universe
does not owe it to us.

Lastly, it is also a story of love unbound---obsession, really---that
may sometimes cause more harm than good.

### Lot's wife

Read [Sodom and Gomorrah Destroyed](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Genesis%2019&version=NIV)

Lot's wife dared to look back on the city of Sodom as it was razed and
leveld by God himself, and she became a pillar of salt.

Lot's wife's "sin" was not actually in the act of looking back,
according to some bible scholars, but in the way she yearned for the old
life she was leaving behind, which, if it were in the city of Sodom, was
a life full of depravity.

The promise of moving on to "greener pastures" can be alluring, but the
transition itself is not always easy. Dealing with addiction comes to
mind; many remain unsuccessful in escaping it. Can life really start
anew if we cannot let go of old habits or keep returning to them? But
also, how ruinous could it be if we dared to "look back"?
