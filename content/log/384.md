## Thoughts on *My Sad Republic*

*Might contain spoilers*.

### Writing style

Eric Gamalinda writes well. This novel's prose speaks in a meandering
way, the way an heirloom is passed around to important people before it
reaches back to the "original" owner, which in itself is a process that
speaks straight to the heart.

### Setting and historical context

*My Sad Republic* is a historical fiction set in the Visayas group of
islands of the so-called [Philippines](/pilipinas), several bodies
of water removed from the Imperial Manila. It follows the fictionalized
life of [Pope Isio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papa_Isio), himself a real person.

Being a historical fiction, the novel refers to real life events. One of
which is the execution of the three Filipino Catholic priests,
[GOMBURZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomburza), by garrote, an awful
Spanish execution method using strangulation. The more awful part was there was no trial for the
priests, not that we should expect any justice system from Spanish
colonizers. (Pepe Diokno, director, adapted the
priests' story into a film, [of which I had some thoughts](/156).)

### Messianic complex and faith

What compels a man---in the novel, it is a man--- to believe and embody
a messianic complex? It is the intersection of multiple factors, much of
which involves luck and charm. Can one be a messiah not despite, but
because of the oppressive environment *he* grew up in?

Isio lectures Asuncion, Isio's childhood sweetheart, about the [Blessed Virgin Mary's Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_of_Mary); that
Mary, Mother of God, was "assumed" into heaven without having to die a
physical death. Isio further mentions the Book of Revelation, in which
"a woman is clothed in sun, with a crown of twelve stars," and says
"it's the Mother of God [but] we're just guessing."

The novel also plays with magical realism. The sudden pop of it can be
used to explain some of Isio's imagination, rumination, and ability to
divine conclusions from speculations. When attached to superstition, it
becomes magical, because how else do we explain what we cannot
understand (yet)?

### Knowledge and pleasure

One of the characters, Doña Leonora Madrigal, a self-assessed benevolent
landlord, thinks that reading books "will make
you blind". Is this effect of accumulating knowledge from minds not
one's own an opportunity cost "one must pay for so much pleasure"?

Address the taste of the body, and you will have a satisfied life.
Sugar, extracted from the land and labor of workers, placates the buds;
and we ask, "More." [Empires](/empire), new and old, have known how to
build power through this extraction. In the future, it will follow the
same formula.

### On love, desire, and the body

At a young age, the two, Isio and Asuncion, encounter hindrances to
their budding relationship. And the familiar feeling of puppy love gone,
never to return, is not lost on me. Before my first real girlfriend, I
was madly infatuated---for it could not have been love at all---with a
girl from my elementary school days. Because of life circumstances, we
were not destined to meet regularly, until one day, they had to migrate
to Canada. It hurt because there was nothing I could have done. The
chance of us getting to know each other shrunk from slim to nonexistent.
Such is young puppy love.

Another familiar feeling: to experience intense orgasms from wild sex is
to experience the void that comes after. Having felt such intensity, the
pangs could only be dreadful when the hollowness finally settles. Sex
does that; not having it invites loneliness and frustration. An empire
could certainly be erected from ten thousand pleasures and desires,
extracted from attractive bodies and dumped onto those plagued by want
of flesh to fornicate. (Frank Herbert's Dune Saga, with its sci-fi
breeding kink ,had already imagined such an empire in the form of a
brutal matriarch called Honored Matres.)

Asuncion is sexually assaulted twice by two different men, both of
whom professed how deep and important their feelings for her. Neither
man knows how to love, and I wonder whether [love](/love) had ever
rooted itself deeper than what the body dictates in the 19th century.

Then and now, jealousy comes from the idea that loving someone means
having them as a property: to be owned, to be discarded. Jealousy is
ingrained in us because love is supposedly a commodity and thus can be
made scarce.

### Power, violence, and contradiction

A wrong way to [raise a child](/having-kids) (and an only child at
that): to isolate him from society early on, only to reveal him to the
public as a king-to-be of a place where he would likely be a tyrant.

Some people will readily brutalize their enemies, dehumanize their
bodies even in death, and then invoke peace as if it is the most logical
conclusion to the horrors they have inflicted.
